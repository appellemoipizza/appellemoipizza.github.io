Millie started from a practical frustration: using more agents was making **me** the message bus. I was still deciding which agent should know what, relaying context, cleaning up branches and remembering what the company had already learned.

The system flips that relationship around. I talk to one operating partner. Millie constructs the smallest useful context, selects relevant specialists or tools, verifies outcomes, and turns results into durable company or project state.

## The interesting part is the boundary

The intelligence is not the model. Models are replaceable workers. Company facts and authority rules belong to the deployment. Evidence belongs in a separate knowledge store. Project repositories remain implementation truth.

That separation makes it possible to change models, execution harnesses and local/cloud routes without pretending the company itself lives inside a prompt.

## What I am testing

- whether a single human-facing coordinator reduces the cognitive cost of agentic work;
- how much context can stay lazy instead of being loaded every session;
- whether Builder → Reviewer → verification produces cleaner autonomous changes;
- when local models are actually qualified enough to replace frontier routes;
- how repeated project work can graduate into reusable capabilities rather than more prompt files.

Millie is also becoming the editorial observer behind this site. When real work produces a lesson, artifact or story, she can say: **I think this deserves a post.** She can draft it. She cannot publish it for me.
