The old version of this site was basically a business card. I liked it, but it had one serious problem: the interesting part of my work was happening behind it.

I have been building products, local AI experiments, internal tools, research systems and eventually Millie—the intelligence I want to use to operate a one-person software company. None of that fits well into six portfolio thumbnails and a list of technologies.

So this is becoming a **personal lab** instead.

There is still selected work. But there is also a Lab for things that do not need to become companies, Notes for ideas that survived contact with implementation, Films for work better shown than explained, and Resources for the rare internal tool that becomes useful enough to clean up for somebody else.

The important rule is that I do not want to become a content machine.

I want the work to create the content.

When a project produces a surprising decision, a useful mechanism, a failure, a visual artifact or a point of view I can actually defend, Millie can surface it in my daily or weekly brief:

> I think this deserves a post.

That sentence is the editorial calendar.

No quota. No invented thread because Tuesday is "content day." Make something real, notice what was learned, and publish when there is a reason.
