I want the things I build to be allowed to have an **other self**.

Not a corporate mascot that gets pasted beside a logo. Something closer to character design: if the program became a creature, what would still have to be true for me to recognise it?

Millie is the first one.

## The invariant

Millie coordinates intelligence that can move through different tools, models and specialist roles while remaining the same entity.

That gives the visual system a useful rule: **Millie can inhabit different situations without losing her silhouette.**

She should read as the same small nocturnal electronic familiar whether she is floating over Tokyo, buried in a workshop, supervising a terminal, thinking, excited, focused or surrounded by other programs.

The character therefore carries architecture, not just personality.

## Why a creature

A dashboard would describe the company. A robot assistant would describe a tool. A human avatar would imply a person.

The creature is stranger and more useful.

It gives Millie enough distance from ordinary software UI to feel like an entity, while staying abstract enough that the intelligence can change underneath it. The eye, body mass and small detached shape do most of the recognition work. Everything else can flex.

## The world matters too

The character sheet is only half of it.

I want each program to accumulate a visual environment around itself: places, objects, weather, materials, recurring compositions, tiny jokes, states and scenes. Millie's world can move between late-night Tokyo, underwater quiet, small electronics, research desks and slightly overbuilt operating rooms.

The point is not consistency for its own sake. The world gives me another medium for explaining what the software feels like and what I was thinking while making it.

## A series, not a launch asset

This becomes a recurring kind of post on this site.

A visual-world entry can contain finished generated scenes, sketches, silhouettes, palette tests, emotional states, failed generations, interface fragments and notes about why one version feels more like the program than another.

Millie is **Visual World 001**.

Next I want to see what happens when Wakomi, huhu, motes, Lexine and Loket are asked the same question:

**if this program woke up inside its own world, what would it look like?**
